# P4: chart ranges and volume

## What was built

- The chart pane has six range tabs on its header's second line: `1D 5D 1M 6M 1Y 5Y`. The lit one is
  the current range. A click on a tab changes the range at once.
- `1D` is the day the board already holds, as before. The other five come from CNBC range files.
- `ranges.rs` (pure): range definitions, the series cache (`Book`, at most 12 series, least recently
  used goes first), the request slots shared with the worker, bar spacing and the time window.
- `source.rs`: `parse_series` reads a range file, and one long-lived `ranges` worker fetches them.
  `parse_chart` (1D) now also reads volume.
- `chartview.rs`: tabs, the long-range buckets (up to a calendar month), date axis, the legend
  stamps, and the volume strip.
- `Bar` has a `v` field (volume, 0 when the feed sends none).

## How to use it

- Tabs: `1D` is the day; `5D` is about a week of intraday bars; `1M` and `6M` show the same file
  (daily bars for about a year), trimmed to 31 or 183 days; `1Y` and `5Y` are daily and longer.
- A tab that is not cached is fetched when it is clicked. The pane says "loading 5D" meanwhile, and
  "could not load 5D, retrying" after a failure. Failures are retried after 30 seconds.
- Refresh: each feed tick asks for whatever is due. A file is refreshed when its time to live has
  passed: 10 minutes for `5D`, an hour for the daily files, six hours for `5Y`. Old data stays on
  screen during a refresh.
- A request that the worker has not picked up is replaced by a newer one (the newest tab wins). A
  request that is presumed lost after 120 seconds is asked again.
- The cache outlives the pane, so returning to a range shows the stored data at once when it is
  still fresh. Closing the chart resets the range to `1D`. Collapsing the panel keeps the range.
- Volume strip: when any candle on screen has volume, its bottom fifth is a volume bar strip in the
  candle's colour, and the price plot is shorter. Without volume (FX, yields, spot metals, or any
  feed that sends none) there is no strip and the pane is as it was.
- Legend: `13:35` on 1D; `Nov 12 13:35` on other intraday ranges; `Nov 12 2026` on daily and longer
  candles. Long candles are bucketed on calendar boundaries: days on midnight, weeks on Monday,
  months on the 1st.
- Whose midnight: intraday bars (1D, 5D) are read on your clock, or on UTC if the header's zone is
  switched. Daily and weekly bars (1M, 6M, 1Y, 5Y) are trading days, so they keep the exchange's own
  date for every viewer, and their header has no zone to switch.

## Decisions the brief did not make

The full list is in `p4-handoff.md`. The finisher added or confirmed these:

- `parse_series` keeps one bar per instant. Where two bars share a time, the later one in the file
  wins. The bar's time is its start, with no 60 s shift and no clamp to now.
- Bars without a finite close are skipped. A non-finite open, high or low falls back to the close.
- `median_spacing` ignores gaps that are zero, negative or not finite (duplicates, unsorted input).
- `Series::window` returns nothing for a negative or NaN span, and everything for an infinite one.

## Changed in review

- Daily and longer series are dated 12 hours east of UTC instead of on the viewer's clock
  (`chartview::date_zone`). A daily bar is stamped at midnight on its exchange's clock, which is
  within 12 hours of the UTC midnight of the same date wherever the exchange is. Read on the viewer's
  clock, every candle was a day early for anyone west of the exchange (Tokyo seen from Manila, New
  York seen from Los Angeles) and Monday's bar fell into the week before. Two tests pin it.
- The header of a daily or longer chart ends at the candle size: no zone name and no zone button.
- `--probe` also prints the last bar's stamp in UTC for each range file, which is where the midnight
  assumption can be seen to hold or not.
- When the symbol list changes, the request that is taken back from the worker's slot is also
  cancelled in the book. Before, a range of a symbol that stayed on the strip could say "loading" for
  up to two minutes.
- The `ranges` worker thread is started on Ultra only. Classic has no chart to ask it for anything.

## Unverified

- Every CNBC range shape is unverified live. The time spelling, whether a bar's stamp is its start or
  its end, the time of day of daily bars, and whether a file carries volume are all taken from the
  one-symbol, one-day measurement in `FEATURES.md`.
- The check is `--probe`: for the first row it fetches each range file and prints the bar count,
  the inferred bar duration, the span in days, whether volume is present and the last bar's stamp
  in UTC. Run it on a machine with network access before trusting the ranges. If the stamps of the
  daily files are not midnight on the exchange's clock, daily candles can be dated a day off.
- The worker, the proxy and the real feed were not exercised from this sandbox. All tests use
  hand-written JSON.
- Pictures (`p4-5d.png`, `p4-1m.png`, `p4-5y.png`, `p4-1d-volume.png`) were rendered from synthetic
  data.
