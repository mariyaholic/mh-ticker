# P6: Type it in

## What was built

- **Symbols > Add symbol...** opens a prompt. The typed symbol is cleaned (trimmed, upper-cased, at most
  20 characters of `A-Z 0-9 . @ = - ^ / : _`), then checked against CNBC with one quote request. A found
  symbol is added to the Symbols menu and to the strip. A symbol already on the strip gets a box that
  says so. A listed but hidden one is ticked.
- **Symbols > Remove a symbol I added** lists the symbols added by hand, as `label` and `symbol`. Removing
  one also removes its alerts, since an alert for a symbol that is gone can never fire. The entry for the
  only shown symbol is greyed, and the strip never goes empty.
- **Up to 24 symbols.** The strip shows at most 24 symbols (it was 12). Each shown symbol adds to the
  quotes fetched on each refresh, so the traffic grows with the number shown. The size of that cost at 24
  was not measured.
- **Alerts > at a price...** opens a prompt that starts from the fresh price. Accepting the current price
  is refused with a reason. A level above the price becomes an above alert, a level below becomes a below
  alert. It honours *Fire once*.
- **Levels on the chart.** Alert levels inside the chart's price window are dashed lines with their price
  at the right end. A level outside the window gives a marker on the nearest edge, with `+n` for the
  others beyond that edge.
- **Right-click on the chart** opens a small menu for the chart: *Alert at* (greyed when there is no
  fresh price or the level would be refused) and *More...*, which opens the main menu.

## How to use it

1. Choose Symbols > Add symbol... from the menu. Type a CNBC symbol such as
   `AAPL`, `EUR=` or `@GC.1`, then press Enter. The name and the first price come from CNBC.
2. To remove it later, choose Symbols > Remove a symbol I added, then pick it.
3. To set a level, choose Alerts > at a price..., or right-click the chart and choose *Alert at*.
   Type a price such as `4,200.50`. A level above the current price alerts when the price rises to it.
   A level below alerts when the price falls to it.

## Decisions

- Labels come from the symbol: a leading `@`, `.` or `^`, a trailing `=` and a trailing `.1` are dropped,
  the rest is cut to 8 characters, and `SYM` is used if nothing is left. A taken label gets `2`, `3` and
  so on.
- Decimal places come from the first price: 0 at 10,000 and above, 2 from 10, 4 from 0.01, and three
  significant figures below that, up to 8.
- A name is cleaned of control characters and whitespace runs, and cut to 40 characters with `...`.
  Tabs and line breaks become spaces, so a name cannot split a menu line.
- A typed level is read with commas as thousands separators, with at most one decimal point. Signs,
  exponents and text are refused. The side rule compares both prices after rounding to the symbol's
  digits.
- The lookup is one request with a 10 second timeout, no redirects and at most 256 KB read. A quote counts
  as found when its symbol equals the typed one, ignoring case, and its price is positive.
- The strip's 24-symbol limit sizes the Symbols menu and the card menu ranges (5600 to 5623 and 5700 to
  5723). The panel's grid for 24 cards is taller than a 1080p work area. The body scrolls.

## Unverified

- The Add symbol and at-a-price dialogs on real Windows. Keyboard behaviour (Enter, Esc, the text
  selected on open) was not checked by hand.
- The lookup against CNBC. What CNBC returns for an unknown symbol is assumed to be no quote, or a quote
  without a positive price. Both read as not found.
- The menus, the pane's right-click menu and the message boxes, which were not clicked through.
- 24 symbols on a real taskbar.
- Under Wine, `prompt::ask` keeps the full initial text when `max_len` is shorter than it, so the cut to
  `max_len` is not proven here. The test for it was left out.
