# P2: a chart that reads (Ultra)

## What it does

The candle pane (Ultra, opened from the chart button) now reads like a chart:

- **Hover legend.** Move the pointer over a candle and a line in the header shows its start time,
  Open, High, Low, Close and the candle's own change, for example
  `01:15  O 56.330  H 56.333  L 56.263  C 56.266  -0.11%`. A crosshair runs through the candle, and a
  horizontal line follows the pointer's price, with the price on the axis. The legend line is always
  reserved, so the plot does not move when the pointer arrives. On a pane too narrow for the full
  line, the legend shows only time, close and change.
- **Local time.** Candles and the time axis are on your local clock by default. The zone name in the
  header ("local" or "UTC") is a button: click it to switch. The choice is saved.
- **Reference lines.** The previous close is a dashed line labelled "prev". The day's high and low are
  short ticks on the right edge of the plot. Each is drawn only when it lies inside the price window
  the candles set.
- **Session breaks.** A faint dotted vertical line marks where the market was shut and trading
  resumed, so a gap is not papered over.
- **Header.** The day's change sits beside the last price, in the up or down colour.

## How to use it

Open the chart pane and move the pointer across the candles. Click "local" or "UTC" in the header to
change the clock. Nothing else is needed.

## Config

One new field in `config.json`:

- `chart_utc` (boolean, default `false`): show the candle pane and its time axis in UTC instead of
  local time. Set by the zone button. A config without the field reads as local time.

## Decisions

From the builder's notes, kept:

- **Legend placement.** The pane header is two lines. Line 1 holds the label, the bucket, the zone
  button, the day change and the last price. Line 2 is the hover legend, reserved when idle.
- **Day change** is dropped (not the zone button) when it would touch the zone button on a narrow pane.
- **Zone button** is the underlined accent-coloured word. The zone flip saves outside the UI
  callback.
- **Zone words:** "local" and "UTC" (`chartview::zone_label`).
- **Reference lines** are drawn under the candles. The previous close is dashed with a "prev" label
  inside the plot at its left edge. The day high and low are 8 px ticks at the right edge, over the
  candles, with no label.
- **Crosshair** is dotted in the accent colour. The hover price tag is drawn after the last-price tag,
  so it may cover it while the pointer is on that row.
- **Pointer quantisation.** The pointer's y is snapped to 2 px bands (scaled by DPI), counted from the
  top of the plot, so a mouse move inside one band does not repaint. The pointer returns nothing
  outside the plot or past the last candle, which clears the crosshair.
- **Stale pointer.** The painter clamps the hovered candle to the candles drawn and ignores a pointer
  y outside the plot, because a resize or a zone flip can refold the candles between frames. A zone
  flip also clears the crosshair.
- **Stored layout.** The layout is cleared at the start of every frame, by early returns, and when the
  hover resets (set_chart, select, new rows, expand). It is also cleared after collapse's animation,
  which paints at other sizes.
- **Fixture.** The chart picture uses a fixed UTC-4 labelled "local", so the PNG is the same on every
  machine. The sixth argument to the example is the hovered candle.

Decisions made in this pass:

- **Gaps and change.** `gaps` refuses a zero, negative, NaN or infinite bucket (returns no breaks).
  `bar_change_pct` returns `None` for a zero or non-finite open, and also for a NaN close, so the
  legend never prints NaN as a change.
- **Readout.** The full legend is `time  O  H  L  C  change`; the change is dropped (with its two
  spaces) when it has no value. The compact legend is `time  C  change`.
- **Doc example fixed.** The doc comment on `readout` said `+0.02%` for the example bar, whose change
  is `+0.01%`. Corrected in the comment only.
- **Lint fix in the painter.** `panel.rs` wrote `y - lh - 1 >= plot.top`, which clippy flags as
  `int_plus_one`. Rewritten as `y - lh > plot.top`. Same comparison for integers, so the same pixels.
  Stills are still 36 of 36 identical.
- **Layout is pure.** `chartview` depends only on `crate::model::Bar` and `std`.
- **One direction, one colour.** The last-price tag and its dotted line follow the day's change, the
  same figure the header and the card show: a change of zero or more is up-coloured, below zero is
  down-coloured. Only when the day's change is unknown or not finite does the colour fall back to the
  bars (last close against first open), as before.
- **Reference-line picture.** The example takes a seventh argument, `refs`. For the pane only, the
  fixture then draws the previous close at the midpoint of the day's range, the day's high and low at
  its extremes, and cuts about forty one-minute bars from the middle so there is one session break.
  The cards keep the normal bars. Without `refs` the fixture is unchanged.

## Known limits (not bugs in the build, but for the reviewer)

- `Layout::y_of(NaN)` returns the bottom of the plot: Rust's float-to-int cast turns NaN into 0. The
  painter never passes a NaN (bars come from JSON), and the code does not guard it. Left as is, since
  the contract says `y_of` is not clamped.
- When the coarsest bucket still has more candles than the plot has pixels, candles are one pixel
  wide and overflow the plot's right edge. That needs a span of several days at one-hour candles in a
  very narrow pane, which the single-day feed does not produce.
- Reference lines follow the rule "drawn only when inside the price window". The window is the bars'
  low and high padded by 8 %. A previous close or day high outside it is not drawn. In the default
  fixture the previous close (56.766) is just above the window, so the default picture has no "prev"
  line. The `refs` picture shows all reference lines.
- The day-low tick and the hover price tag in the `refs` picture are about 6 px apart on the right
  edge. They do not overlap at this size, but they are close.
- The compact legend only appears on a pane narrower than the full line. The 1130 px render at 144
  DPI fits the full line, so the compact one is covered by a unit test only.

## Tests

- `chartview` unit tests: 26, all passing.
- Whole suite (`wcargo test --all-features --no-fail-fast`): 128 passed, 1 failed, 0 ignored. The one
  failure is `ui::tests::a_theme_switch_takes_the_new_themes_face`, which Wine cannot run (no Consolas
  font). Not caused by this package.
- Ignored tests: none remain.
- Clippy (`--all-features --all-targets`): no warnings except the expected `windres not found`.
- `scripts/check-state-linux.sh`: passes.
- `stills-check`: 36 of 36 identical to the baseline.

New edge-case tests cover: no bars, one bar, all bars identical (flat), NaN and zero opens and closes,
a pointer on the plot's left and right edges and one pixel outside, `index_at(x_of(i)) == i` for every
candle at several plot widths and DPIs (96, 120, 144, 192), `price_at(y_of(p))` within one pixel of
price, folding with +05:30 and -03:30 landing on local hours, the clock with offsets and midnight
wrap, and offset 0 folding exactly as before zones existed.

## Unverified

Nothing here was checked with a real mouse on a real Windows desktop. Specifically:

- **Reviewed by code only:** the hover on real hardware (pointer capture, mouse leave, the 2 px
  quantisation under real mouse movement), the click on the zone button, and the save of `chart_utc`.
- **Reviewed by code and by rendered PNG only:** the layout, the legend text, the crosshair position
  and the header at 96 and 144 DPI. The PNGs are rendered under Wine with substitute fonts, so text
  widths and line heights differ from a Windows machine. Check the legend fits and does not overlap
  with the real font.
- **Daylight saving:** the offset is read with `GetTimeZoneInformation` on each paint, so a clock
  change applies on the next repaint. Bars already fetched are refolded then, not cached. Not tested
  across a real change.
